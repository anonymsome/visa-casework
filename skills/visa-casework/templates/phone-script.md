# Template: district office phone call

Use one to two business days **after** submitting, so there is a case to
reference. Call the **district** office, not D.C. — casework lives in the
district.

About thirty seconds. The goal is to put a voice on a file that is otherwise
one of many, and to walk away with a name.

---

"Hi — my name is [FULL NAME], I'm a constituent in [CITY/NEIGHBORHOOD].

I submitted a casework request online on [DATE] about a visa appointment
issue. I'm calling to confirm it came through and to ask who's handling it.

Short version: I'm [WORKING/STUDYING] here on [STATUS], my status is valid,
but my visa stamp has expired and there's no appointment available at
[POST] — the earliest is [DATE]. So I can't leave the country and come
back. [ONE SENTENCE ON WHY TRAVEL MATTERS.]

I'm not asking anyone to approve a visa — I know that's not something the
office can do. I'm hoping you can inquire with the State Department about
appointment availability and whether I'd qualify for an expedite."

---

## Write down

- The caseworker's name and direct extension
- The case or file number
- What they said the next step is, and when to check back

## If asked

- **Address / district:** have it ready; they verify constituency first.
- **Documents:** yes, and ask how they want them sent. Do not read
  identifiers over the phone unless they ask for them.
- **Have you contacted another office:** answer honestly. If a senator's
  office is also involved, say so — offices coordinate, and a surprise later
  reads badly.

## If it goes to voicemail

Name, callsback number, "I submitted a casework request on [DATE] about a
visa appointment issue and wanted to confirm it was received." Keep it to
twenty seconds and call again in two days rather than leaving a second
message.
