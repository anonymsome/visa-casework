# What a congressional office can actually do

State this honestly at the start. A user who expects a visa and gets an
inquiry feels misled; a user who expects an inquiry and gets one is served.

## Can

- Open a **congressional inquiry** with the State Department's Bureau of
  Consular Affairs. There is an established liaison channel for member
  offices.
- Ask about **appointment availability** at a specific post, and whether
  anything can be done about a wait.
- **Transmit an expedite request** and ask that it be considered against
  published criteria.
- Get a **status answer** on a case in administrative processing — often
  only that it remains pending, which is still more than the portal gives.
- Sometimes **surface an option the user did not know about**: third-country
  processing, an interview waiver they qualify for, a post with movement.
- Create a **record and a named human**. A case number and a caseworker who
  knows the file changes what a follow-up call can accomplish.

## Cannot

- Issue, approve, or expedite a visa by their own authority.
- Direct a consular officer's decision. Adjudication is insulated from
  outside influence by design, and an office that tried would be acting
  improperly.
- Overturn a refusal. Consular nonreviewability is close to absolute; State
  headquarters does not overrule an officer on the merits either.
- Create an appointment slot that does not exist.
- Move faster than State's liaison channel, which runs on weeks.
- Help a non-constituent. Offices serve their own district.

## The honest ceiling

An inquiry converts an unanswerable situation into an answered one, and
occasionally shakes loose an appointment or an expedite. It is not a lever
on the outcome. Worth filing — it is free, it is routine, and almost nobody
in this situation uses it — and worth filing with accurate expectations.

## Timing

Days to weeks for acknowledgment; weeks for a substantive answer from State.
Two weeks of silence is normal, not a signal to escalate. Escalate to a
senator after a genuine non-response, and sequentially — offices say
duplicate simultaneous inquiries do not speed a case and can tangle it.
