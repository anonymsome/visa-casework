# Template: escalating to a senator

**Only after a genuine non-response from the House office.** Roughly four to
six weeks with no substantive reply, after at least one follow-up and one
phone call.

This is sequential, not parallel. Offices state that duplicate simultaneous
inquiries do not speed a case and can tangle it — two offices querying the
same State liaison about the same file produces confusion, not pressure. If
the House office is actively working the case, wait.

Disclose the prior inquiry. It is asked on the form, offices find out
anyway, and concealing it is the one move that damages a case.

Senators serve the whole state, so establish constituency by city.

---

Dear Senator [LAST NAME],

I am a constituent in [CITY, STATE]. I am writing to ask your office to
inquire with the Department of State about a consular appointment issue that
has left me unable to travel.

I first contacted Representative [NAME]'s office on [DATE]
[WITH CASE NUMBER IF ANY]. [WHAT HAPPENED — acknowledged then silent, or no
response at all, or an answer that did not address the issue. Factual, not
aggrieved.] I am approaching your office because that inquiry has not
produced a response, not to run a second request alongside it.

[THE SITUATION — condensed from the original request. Status category, how
long in the U.S., employer or school, which post, what the wait is, what has
been tried.]

[WHAT IS ON THE OTHER SIDE — the reason travel matters. Update it if
anything has changed since the first filing; a worsened situation is the
strongest thing in this letter.]

As before, I am not asking anyone to approve a visa or influence a consular
decision. I am asking whether your office can inquire about appointment
availability at [POST] and whether my circumstances warrant expedited
consideration.

I am glad to sign a Privacy Act release for your office and to share
everything I sent previously.

Sincerely,
[FULL NAME]
[STREET ADDRESS]
[CITY, STATE, ZIP]
[PHONE] / [EMAIL]

---

## Notes

- A senator's office needs its own Privacy Act release. The House office's
  does not carry over.
- Two senators per state: pick one. Filing with both at once is the same
  duplicate-inquiry problem one level up.
- If the House office responds after this goes out, tell the senator's
  office.
